Executive Summary

Modern healthcare infrastructure faces an acute dual crisis: clinical workflow congestion and diagnostic triage latency. In emergency and outpatient clinics, delayed patient assessment often leads to preventable clinical deterioration, whereas physicians spend up to 45% of their daily routine on administrative charting and prescription reconciliation.

MediPulse AI is an intelligent, scalable, and decentralized clinical automation platform designed to bridge the gap between patient self-reporting, automated urgency triaging, real-time physician tele-consultation, and synchronized Electronic Health Record (EHR) management. Built upon robust Object-Oriented Software Engineering principles and governed by an Agile Scrum methodology, this capstone project delivers:

  • A machine-assisted clinical decision support triage engine categorizing patient urgency into standardized levels (Critical, Urgent, Routine) and routing patients to matching medical specialists.
  • A comprehensive Unified Modeling Language (UML) structural and behavioral specification comprising Data Flow Diagrams (Level 0 and 1), Use Case Diagrams, Activity Diagrams, Entity-Relationship Diagrams (ERD), Class Diagrams, and Object Diagrams.
  • A complete, working, object-oriented prototype implementing at least six core domain classes (User, Patient, Doctor, Appointment, MedicalRecord, Prescription, and TriageEngine) with interactive web and console execution interfaces.
  • An empirical project completion timeline featuring a Work Breakdown Structure (WBS), sprint milestones, deliverables matrix, and a production Gantt chart.

1. Introduction & Project Overview

1.1 Background & Motivation

The global healthcare ecosystem is experiencing unprecedented operational strain. Escalating chronic disease prevalence, coupled with physician shortages, has led to prolonged outpatient waiting times and clinical burnout. Traditional Hospital Information Systems (HIS) and Electronic Health Record (EHR) platforms are often monolithic, rigid, and disconnected from the patient's immediate triage journey. Patients frequently experience confusion regarding which medical specialty to consult, leading to inappropriate emergency room visits or dangerous delays in acute intervention.

1.2 Problem Statement

Existing clinical management software architectures suffer from three critical bottlenecks:

  1. Subjective and Delayed Triage: Patient symptom self-reporting lacks systematic urgency stratification prior to physical or tele-health check-in, resulting in suboptimal resource allocation.
  2. Data Fragmentation in Outpatient Encounters: Diagnostic findings, dynamic physiological vitals, and therapeutic prescriptions are frequently segregated across disjoint databases, requiring manual duplicate data entry by physicians.
  3. Lack of Modern, Modular Object-Oriented Extensibility: Legacy platforms struggle to integrate modern AI-assisted diagnostic assistants and mobile microservices due to tightly-coupled procedural architectures.

1.3 Proposed Solution

MediPulse AI addresses these challenges through a modular, object-oriented, patient-centric ecosystem. The system features:

  • An automated Triage Evaluation Engine assessing symptom acuity and vital signs.
  • Dynamic Physician Matching and Appointment Dispatch prioritizing acute patients.
  • An integrated Clinical Encounter Workspace enabling physicians to review vitals, document diagnoses, and digitally sign structured prescriptions in a unified flow.
  • A cohesive Electronic Health Record (EHR) Repository persisting immutable clinical histories.

5. Prototype Implementation & Interface

In compliance with the assignment specification ("Develop a simple interface using at least 5 classes that are in the class diagrams"), the prototype explicitly instantiates, manipulates, and links 6 domain classes:

  1. Patient: Encapsulates patient demographics, triage history, and active records.
  2. Doctor: Models clinical credentials, specialty, fees, and consultation authority.
  3. Appointment: Manages scheduling slots, acuity priorities, and status updates.
  4. MedicalRecord: Persists formal clinical findings, diagnosis, and physiological vitals.
  5. Prescription: Structures pharmacological orders, dosages, and fulfillment tracking.
  6. TriageEngine: Algorithmic symptom evaluation and automatic specialist matching.
